## Two things that cost time this tick

**`timeout` does not exist on macOS.** `timeout 900 git push …` failed with
`command not found`, and the `echo "EXIT=$?"` that followed reported `EXIT=0` — because it was
reporting `echo`'s status, not the push's. The push had not happened. The only reason this was
caught is that the same command printed LOCAL and REMOTE SHAs and they differed. Keep comparing
LOCAL to REMOTE after every push; `feedback_git_commit_phantom` is still earning its keep.

**`orca orchestration run-bind` is not a command.** The verb is `run-use`, and it takes
`--id`, not `--run`.

**Rebinding the Run fences the previous consumer, and that breaks launcher cleanup.**
`orca orchestration run-use --id run_0950f6f54b72` bumped `consumer_generation` to 19. Afterwards
`orca-headless.rb cleanup --state-dir … --settlement-message msg_26d55ef8d3aa` refused with
`consumer_fenced`: it verifies settlement through that `worker_done` message, and the message
belongs to the fenced generation.

That fencing is deliberate — it stops an old receipt from authorising cleanup of a newer
terminal — but the consequence is that **lanes started under a previous tick's binding cannot be
cleaned up by the launcher after a rebind.** Their `runner.rb` wrappers keep holding their PTYs;
that is where the 73 zombies on this machine come from, and the oldest wrappers are two days old.

Not forced. The launcher's own contract says an ambiguous cleanup stage is the coordinator's
manual call and that residual resources must not be deleted, so the directories and wrappers were
left intact. `orca orchestration worker-release` *did* succeed for all six dispatches, so Orca's
own accounting is settled; only the launcher-side terminal close is outstanding.

Whoever picks this up: decide once whether to (a) teach cleanup to accept a current-generation
settlement proof, (b) run cleanup for a lane *before* rebinding the Run, or (c) accept the wrapper
leak and reap it out of band. Option (b) costs nothing and is available today — clean up last
tick's lanes **before** calling `run-use`.
